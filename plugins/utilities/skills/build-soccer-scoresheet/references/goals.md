# Goals: assists and descriptions

Read this when a report has a goal. Each goal line names the scorer, the
assist when there is one, and, when a source shows it, a short description
of the finish.

## Assists

The Laws of the Game do not define an assist, and leagues and data
providers differ on the edges. Use these common rules:

- **The final pass.** A pass, cross, or through ball from a teammate that
  leads directly to the goal is an assist. "Directly" means the scorer
  needs no more than a touch or two to control and shoot; a long dribble
  past defenders after the pass breaks the link.
- **Rebounds.** When a teammate's shot hits the post or crossbar, or is
  saved, and the scorer finishes the rebound right away, the shooter gets
  the assist.
- **Deflections.** A pass that brushes an opponent and still reaches the
  intended teammate is still an assist. A loose ball that an opponent
  clears, miskicks, or loses in a tackle is not one, unless a teammate then
  plays it to the scorer.
- **No assist** for a goal scored directly from a penalty, direct free
  kick, or corner kick, or for winning the penalty a teammate converts.
  Own goals have no assist.

Write the assist in the goal line: `Goal: Name #N (Assisted by Name #N)`.
Credit one only when a source supports it: the user's notes or a coach
message, a team app or tracker, or the recording. When the recording shows
it, the passer's number has to be legible and match the roster, as for any
gap filled from the video; append ` (from video)`. When the recording shows
no teammate's pass (a solo run, a mistake by the opponent, a scramble where
the last touch cannot be seen), leave the assist off and say so in the
reply rather than guessing.

## Describing the finish

When the notes or the recording show how a goal was scored, add a short
description after the scorer and assist, before any video link:

`- **8'** - ⚽ Goal: Name #13 (Assisted by Name #32). Shot from the right of the box, high into the left side of the net; the keeper didn't touch it. ([video](link))`

Cover what the source shows, in a sentence or two:

- **Where the shot came from**: distance (close range, edge of the box,
  from distance) and side (left, right, center), or the set piece (corner,
  free kick, penalty).
- **How it was struck**: first time, header, volley, rebound, chip, or
  after a dribble past a defender.
- **Where it went in**: top or bottom, left, right, or center, seen from
  the shooter's side of the goal; a keeper diving the wrong way or a goal
  off the post is worth saying.
- **The keeper**: whether the keeper got a touch on it.

Do not describe what you cannot see. From a distant camera the side and
height of the finish are usually clear while the player's number is not;
write what is clear and leave the rest out. A description from the user's
notes ("header", "screamer from 30 yards") uses their words.
